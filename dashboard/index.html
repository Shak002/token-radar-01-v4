Implied value is this radar's own claim from the snapshot's figures — one joint regression of market cap on TVL and age, fitted across all live tokens (a token's market cap grows with TVL to the power 0.52 in this snapshot, not one-for-one), plus a per-category offset, then nudged by seven-day growth and chain breadth — never a prediction that a token will launch, or when. These protocols already have a token that trades, so the board never ranks them. Their market cap is the market's own value, not a model.

How the radar scores a protocol

Every row is scored by the same formula, from the snapshot alone. No name is ever special-cased. The base value is one joint regression, fitted by least squares across all live-token protocols in the snapshot: log10(market cap) on log10(TVL) and log10(age in days), plus a per-category offset. TVL does the work — a protocol with ten times the TVL is worth about three times the token, not ten times — and age on its own is nearly flat, so the model does not assume an older tokenless protocol is worth more; it lets the snapshot say so.

The regression. In this snapshot the fit is log10(market cap) = 3.33 + 0.52 × log10(TVL) − 0.10 × log10(age in days). The offset is the shrunk mean of the category's residuals, each residual clipped to ±1.0 in log10 and the offset capped at ±0.5, so one protocol cannot move its whole category — this snapshot holds one prediction-market token at 400× its TVL, and without the clipping it would have priced every prediction market far above the curve; with it, the category's offset is about +0.20, about 1.6×.

Why one regression, not two estimators. The previous model averaged a category multiple with this curve. On the snapshot's own live tokens the multiple is the weak half: it prices a $2.7B curator at the same multiple as a $5M one, and the held-out check says the regression alone beats the average. So the multiple is gone; the regression is the value, and the small nudges below are the only other terms.

The nudges. Growth and breadth are deliberately small and not fitted: seven-day growth is clamped to −50%…+120% and weighted 0.5 (a protocol that grew 120% in a week earns 1.6×; one that fell 50% earns 0.75×), and each extra chain up to eight adds 3%. Fitted on these 128 live tokens, a growth term comes out at −1.63 with a standard error of 1.00 — the snapshot cannot tell whether fast TVL growth helps or hurts a token's multiple — so growth stays a stated nudge rather than a fitted sledgehammer that would price a fast grower at 1% of its curve value. The board's top row is $163M, inside the range the snapshot's own live tokens occupy.


    

    
Checked, not just fitted

    

    
The check is a calibration check, not a proof. It says how well the model predicts tokens that already trade, so the board's claims about tokens that do not exist yet are grounded in something measured. Its limit is honest too: the snapshot's own live tokens span market-cap/TVL from 0.003 to 400, so no model of this size predicts a single token closely — what the check shows is that the calibration is real and the ranking is sound.

    
What "pre-token" means

    
A protocol is pre-token when its DefiLlama record carries no market cap, no CoinGecko id and no symbol. The watchlist is the floor: every protocol at or above $50,000,000 TVL that records no token and is not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a token that already trades. The board adds every other rankable pre-token protocol the list leaves out, above or below that floor, by TVL — the early ones the radar exists to find. Six product lines are left out because DefiLlama records them under a protocol whose token already trades (Solv, IPOR, Circle, GMX and Pendle).

    
How to read a farm plan

    
Each row's plan is six steps built from the protocol's own record — its category, its chains, its TVL and its age. Every step states what it costs and how long it takes, with gas priced for the protocol's own first chain, not a flat rate; the second step carries the one guardrail for its category, and the third names the weekly amount. Never pay to claim: a real claim costs gas only, and only from a link the protocol itself publishes.
